# AIDB Channel Distribution: A Day-Zero Observation

**Author:** Noel Santayana
**Built for:** AIDB Growth Engineer application (May 2026)
**Frame:** Observation, not prescription. Outsider read with limited data.

---

## A personal note before the analysis

I listen to AIDB on Spotify. I only went to YouTube to source the episode for this application. The first thing I noticed was the view-to-subscriber gap. This page is the surface-level read I would want to validate with internal data on day one, not a strategy I am proposing from the outside.

## The observation

AIDB's YouTube channel has roughly 582,000 subscribers. Recent daily episodes pull around 5,000 to 14,000 views each. That is a view-to-subscriber ratio of about 1 to 2.5 percent. For active channels, a healthy ratio is usually 10 percent or higher.

The gap is the signal. Not the absolute view count, but the delta between the subscriber base and per-video reach on the daily cadence.

## What the outliers prove

The same channel that pulls 8K on a daily news post pulls dramatically more on visually structured deep-dives:

| Views | Episode |
|---|---|
| 2.8M | How to Design an AI Native Engineering Organization |
| 262K | Moltbook, the Agent Social Network, is the Craziest AI Phenomena Yet |
| 159K | AI Reveals Text In Ancient Greek Scroll Destroyed in Vesuvius Eruption |
| 152K | 25 Things Nano Banana Pro Does That AI Couldn't Before |
| 140K | AI New Year's: The 10 Week AI Resolution |
| 131K | How AI Starts Doing the Work in 2026 with Anthropic CPO Mike Krieger |

These are not outliers because the audience changed. They are outliers because the format changed. Visual, structured, evergreen.

The audience for AIDB's best YouTube work clearly exists. The platform's algorithm just is not being trained to find it under the weight of daily talking-head news posts.

## The hypothesis (to validate, not prescribe)

The daily news cadence likely fights YouTube's algorithm. YouTube optimizes for retention and click-through rate. Daily talking-head news drives both down. Viewers skip days, the algorithm reads disinterest, and subsequent uploads get suppressed even for actively subscribed users.

The non-prescription read: AIDB's YouTube channel could probably be re-positioned as the home for the format the back catalog already proves works (visual, evergreen, weekly-paced deep-dives), while the daily news beat stays where its audience already is (Spotify, Apple Podcasts, RSS).

I would not propose this as a strategy from the outside. I would propose it as a hypothesis to test against internal data the team has and I do not.

## Questions for the team

These are the questions that would make this observation actionable instead of armchair:

1. **What is the current view-distribution by episode type on YouTube?** (Daily news vs. deep-dive vs. interview.) Confirms or refutes the format-mismatch hypothesis.
2. **What share of total AIDB consumption happens on YouTube vs. audio platforms?** If YouTube is a single-digit share of total listens, optimizing it is lower-leverage than optimizing audio-platform sharing.
3. **Has cadence or format experimentation been tried on YouTube before?** What happened, and what made it revert (if it did)?
4. **What is the editorial constraint?** Is "daily" a brand promise or a workflow default? A weekly-evergreen-on-YouTube pivot is only viable if the daily promise can stay anchored elsewhere.
